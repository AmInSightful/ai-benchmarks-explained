# AI Product Roadmapping

> **AI product roadmapping is the process of planning AI initiatives while balancing customer value, technical uncertainty, experimentation, and business impact.**

---

## 🎯 What You'll Learn

After reading this chapter, you will understand:

- Why AI roadmaps are different from traditional product roadmaps
- How to structure an AI roadmap
- How to prioritize AI opportunities
- How to manage uncertainty
- How to move from experiments to scalable products

---

# Why AI Roadmaps Are Different

Traditional software roadmap:

```
Requirement

↓

Design

↓

Development

↓

Release
```

---

AI roadmap:

```
Problem Discovery

↓

Experimentation

↓

Evaluation

↓

Learning

↓

Productization

↓

Scale
```

---

# Why?

Because AI introduces uncertainty:

- Will the model perform well?
- Is the data good enough?
- Will users trust the output?
- Will costs be sustainable?

---

# The AI Roadmap Principle

The goal is not:

> "Build AI features as quickly as possible."

The goal is:

> "Reduce uncertainty while creating customer value."

---

# The AI Product Roadmap Framework

A practical AI roadmap has five stages:

```
1. Discover

↓

2. Validate

↓

3. Build

↓

4. Launch

↓

5. Scale
```

---

# Stage 1: Discovery

## Goal

Find valuable AI opportunities.

---

Questions:

- What user problem exists?
- Where is manual effort high?
- Where can AI create leverage?

---

Examples:

Customer support:

Problem:

Agents spend hours searching answers.

---

Opportunity:

AI knowledge assistant.

---

# Discovery Activities

Use:

- User interviews
- Workflow analysis
- Data analysis
- Competitive research

---

# Stage 2: Validation

## Goal

Test whether AI can solve the problem.

---

Do not start with engineering.

Start with experiments.

---

Examples:

- Prompt experiments
- Prototype
- Human-in-the-loop testing
- Small user trials

---

# Validation Questions

Ask:

## User Value

Do users find it useful?

---

## AI Quality

Are outputs good enough?

---

## Business Value

Does it improve outcomes?

---

## Technical Feasibility

Can we build it?

---

# Stage 3: Build MVP

## Goal

Create the smallest useful AI product.

---

An AI MVP should include:

```
Core User Workflow

+

AI Capability

+

Feedback Collection

+

Quality Measurement
```

---

# Example

AI Sales Assistant MVP:

Not:

"Fully autonomous sales agent."

---

Instead:

"Generate sales email drafts using customer information."

---

# Stage 4: Launch

AI launches should usually be gradual.

---

Example:

```
Internal Testing

↓

Employee Beta

↓

Selected Customers

↓

Public Launch
```

---

# Why Gradual Launch Matters

It helps teams discover:

- Unexpected failures
- User behavior
- Cost problems
- Trust issues

---

# Stage 5: Scale

After validation:

Improve:

- Accuracy
- Speed
- Cost
- Reliability
- User adoption

---

# AI Roadmap Horizons

A useful approach:

## Horizon 1: Improve Existing Product

Timeline:

0-3 months

---

Examples:

- AI summaries
- AI search
- AI recommendations

---

## Horizon 2: Create New Workflows

Timeline:

3-12 months

---

Examples:

- AI assistants
- AI copilots
- AI automation

---

## Horizon 3: Transform Business Model

Timeline:

12+ months

---

Examples:

- Autonomous agents
- AI-native products
- New revenue streams

# Prioritizing AI Initiatives

Traditional prioritization is not enough.

Consider:

```
Customer Value

+

AI Feasibility

+

Business Impact

+

Strategic Advantage

+

Risk
```

---

# AI Opportunity Score

Example:

| Factor | Score |
|-|-|
| User Pain | High |
| AI Capability | High |
| Data Availability | Medium |
| Business Impact | High |
| Risk | Medium |

---

# Managing AI Uncertainty

AI projects often have unknowns.

Examples:

"We don't know if AI quality is good enough."

---

Instead of asking:

"Can we build it?"

Ask:

"What experiment reduces uncertainty?"

---

# Experiment-Driven Roadmaps

Replace:

```
Feature Commitment
```

with:

```
Learning Commitment
```

---

Example:

Bad roadmap item:

"Build AI assistant."

---

Better:

"Validate whether AI assistant can reduce support workload by 30%."

---

# AI Roadmap Metrics

Track three categories:

---

# Learning Metrics

Are we reducing uncertainty?

Examples:

- Evaluation scores
- Prototype results
- User feedback

---

# Product Metrics

Are users getting value?

Examples:

- Adoption
- Engagement
- Retention

---

# Business Metrics

Does it create impact?

Examples:

- Revenue
- Cost reduction
- Productivity

---

# Common AI Roadmapping Mistakes

---

# Mistake 1: Treating AI Like Normal Features

AI requires experimentation.

---

# Mistake 2: Promising Too Early

AI capability may change.

---

# Mistake 3: Building Before Validating

A prototype can reveal major problems.

---

# Mistake 4: Ignoring Operational Costs

Successful AI adoption can increase costs quickly.

---

# Mistake 5: Roadmap Based on Technology Hype

Build because:

"AI is popular."

Not because:

"Users need it."

---

## Strategy

☐ What problem are we solving?

---

## Validation

---

## Data

---

## Evaluation

---

## Economics

☐ Can this scale?

---

## Adoption

# The Mental Model

An AI roadmap is not:

```
Ideas

↓

Features

↓

Launch
```

It is:

```
Problems

↓

Experiments

↓

Learning

↓

Validated Solutions

↓

Scaled Products
```

---

# Key Takeaways

- AI roadmaps should prioritize learning, not only delivery.
- Experimentation is a core part of AI product management.
- Successful AI products reduce uncertainty before scaling.
- AI initiatives should balance value, feasibility, economics, and risk.
- The best AI roadmaps evolve as teams learn.
